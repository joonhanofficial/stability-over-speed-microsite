A rebound number is not the same as recovery quality.

Focusing only on the headline rebound speed can hide the structural pressure underneath. To understand the full picture, we must look beyond the first signal.

Hover or tap cards to reveal diagnostic meaning.

Headline Signal

The immediate, visible recovery number typically measured by GDP growth after a shock.

Diagnostic meaning

Headline Signal

The rebound. It shows speed and direction but does not measure the cost or the stability of the movement.

Pressure Signal

The secondary data point—often inflation—that indicates the internal friction of the recovery.

Diagnostic meaning

Pressure Signal

The friction. It shows whether the recovery is overheating the economy or creating new instabilities.

Better Reading

The combined analytical view that weighs the rebound against the pressure signal.

Diagnostic meaning

Better Reading

The quality. It distinguishes between a stable, sustainable return to growth and a volatile, high-pressure rebound.

From raw public files to an analysis-ready comparison table.

The project used SQL in BigQuery to clean, reshape, and join raw World Bank GDP and inflation files into a country-year analytical table suitable for Tableau visualization.

  1. Imported raw World Bank GDP and inflation files
  2. Filtered selected countries and years
  3. Reshaped wide-format year columns into long country-year records
  4. Standardized country and year fields
  5. Joined GDP growth and inflation into one analytical table
  6. Validated row count, country scope, year scope, and missing values
  7. Exported the processed CSV for Tableau visualization
Final table

A stronger reading, not a complete economic model.

This descriptive analysis improves on a single-metric view, but it remains a simplified comparison tool.

What it improves

  • Adds a second dimension (inflation) to the headline rebound signal.
  • Highlights the friction of the recovery, not just the speed.
  • Standardizes country data for easier cross-economy comparison.

What it does not cover

  • Does not establish causal links between specific policies and outcomes.
  • Excludes other critical factors like debt levels, employment, or trade.
  • Does not predict future growth or account for regional shocks.

The Shift

Moving from measuring speed to measuring stability changes how we value economic success.

By grounding analysis in multiple signals, we move closer to understanding the true quality of a recovery.
